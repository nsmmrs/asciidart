# ADR-0014: Math in Every Format

**Status:** Final. Decided on 2026-10-07.

## Context

`stem:[]` and stem blocks hold AsciiMath or LaTeX. Asciidoctor's HTML
leaves them to MathJax in the browser. Its DocBook converter and
asciidoctor-epub3 turn AsciiMath into MathML with the `asciimath` gem
when it is installed (and warn "AsciiMath to MathML conversion is not
available" when it isn't); LaTeX stays source. asciidoctor-pdf renders
neither without asciidoctor-mathematical, a native extension (a TeX math
engine and an SVG renderer). asciidart has no AsciiMath converter: its
DocBook and EPUB give the warning, its PDF prints the source.

## Decision

1. **AsciiMath to MathML in Dart**, a port of the `asciimath` gem 2.0.6
   (MIT): the same parser and the same MathML, byte for byte, checked
   against the gem on its own test expressions. The DocBook and EPUB
   backends use it as Asciidoctor and asciidoctor-epub3 do with the gem
   installed (`<mml:math>` in DocBook's `<inlineequation>` and
   `<informalequation>`; MathML in the EPUB's content documents, which
   declare the `mathml` property). The parity references install the gem
   too, so they compare the same thing.

2. **LaTeX math stays source** in DocBook and EPUB (as in Asciidoctor and
   asciidoctor-epub3).

3. **Math in the PDF is its own project** (lane epic, named in its cards):
   laying out MathML needs a math font with an OpenType MATH table (Noto
   Sans Math, STIX Two Math) and TeX's rules for fractions, scripts,
   radicals and stretchy delimiters. It is planned in phases: AsciiMath's
   MathML first (the converter above gives it), LaTeX after. Until then
   the PDF sets math in the code font, as its source, and warns once per
   document that it does.

## Consequences

- `doc/formats.md`'s math row: DocBook and EPUB have MathML for AsciiMath;
  the PDF's gap is the epic.
- The "AsciiMath to MathML conversion is not available" warning goes away
  in DocBook and EPUB.

## Update (2026-10-07): AsciiMath in the PDF

Point 3's first phase is done:

- **Layout in libpdf.** libpdf reads the OpenType `MATH` table (constants,
  italic corrections, accent attachments, variants and glyph assemblies),
  parses Presentation MathML, and lays it out by the table's rules and
  MathML Core's (TeX's): the same MathML the DocBook and EPUB backends
  write, so the three formats agree on what a formula is.
- **The font.** Noto Sans Math is bundled (OFL, 990 KB in the native
  executable): its coverage is complete for what AsciiMath writes, and
  its license allows bundling. It is a sans: a theme with a serif text
  face may prefer STIX Two Math or Libertinus Math
  (`math_font_family`, a family of the font catalog whose font has a
  `MATH` table; anything else is reported and Noto Sans Math used).
  Characters the math font lacks come from the base font.
- **Inline and display.** Inline formulas are set at the text's size,
  standing on the baseline with their depth below it (the line grows as
  needed), in the text's color; STEM blocks are in display style,
  centered by default (`stem_text_align`), scaled down when wider than the
  room. Copied, a formula gives its AsciiMath source (`ActualText`), not
  the math alphabet's characters.
- **LaTeX** is converted to MathML by asciidart (`lib/src/math/latex.dart`:
  the math-mode commands KaTeX and MathJax have in common, the amsmath
  environments documents use) and set by the same layout; a command it
  doesn't know is shown as written and reported. In DocBook and EPUB,
  LaTeX stays source, as the gems keep it (point 2): the converter could
  give them MathML too, a decision for when someone asks.
